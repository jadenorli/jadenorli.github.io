About Me

marine science • painting • scientific videography

I’m Jaden Orli, a marine ecologist trained in biology at the College of Creative Studies, University of California, Santa Barbara. I work where field programs, population models, and public-facing communication meet, so that decisions land with both policymakers and communities.

Modeling and Code

Stage-Structured Slot Limits and MPAs Sensitivity and Uncertainty Geospatial Mapping

A two-sex, stage-structured lobster model with von Bertalanffy growth and a size-to-fecundity relationship. Fishing is applied after projection and only outside marine protected areas (MPAs), with scenario sweeps across slot width and marine protected area coverage.

More Modeling Details

Framework

A female-first stage matrix with optional male coupling, exponential natural mortality, and quarterly time steps of dt = 0.25. Harvest is accounted for cleanly through post-projection survival in cells outside marine protected areas, so that harvest is the difference between the raw and the fished trajectories.

Uncertainty and Robustness

  • Sobol global sensitivity analysis, run with the Salt package, to rank which parameters carry the most leverage
  • Generalized Likelihood Uncertainty Estimation ensembles, to show plausible ranges rather than a single fit
  • Reproducible Quarto reports, tidy pipelines, and consistent figure styling

Personal

Photography

Underwater Microscopy Coast and Portraits

Most of my photography happens either on a dive or down a microscope. The range is wider than it sounds: the same interest in structure that makes a kelp canopy worth shooting is what makes a copepod worth shooting, just at a different working distance.

More on Photography

Underwater: kelp forest canopies, reef structure, and the animals that hold still long enough on a low-visibility dive.

Microscopy: copepods, parasites, and the fine anatomy behind the figures in my papers. Several of these started as working images and turned out to be worth printing.

Above water: coastlines, working harbors, and portraits of the people doing the work.

Painting

Wildlife Portraits Coastal Landscapes Mixed Media

I paint in oil and mixed media, mostly places I have been in the water and animals I have spent time around. It is the only part of my practice with no data in it, which is most of the appeal.

More on Painting

Subjects: coastal bays, trout streams, places I know well, and animals with a story attached, sometimes literal and sometimes not.

How it works: some paintings are observational and some are assembled from field impressions months after the fact. The assembled ones usually turn out better.

Why it matters: painting is slow in a way that fieldwork and code are not, and it is where I actually learned how light, pattern, and atmosphere behave. That reading carries straight into figure design.

Trails, Diving, and Travel

Coastal Hikes Night and Low Visibility Working Harbors

Most weekends I’m on a coastal trail or underwater, and travel tends to follow coastlines and working harbors rather than cities.

More on Trails and Travel

Hiking: coastal bluffs, estuary loops, and any trail that ends at a tidepool. A pocket notebook usually comes along for sketches.

Diving: roughly three quarters of my logged dives are recreational rather than scientific, and the habits transfer in both directions. Kelp holdfasts, night entries, and low-visibility marsh work all reward the same discipline.

Travel: I gravitate to places where ecology, fisheries, and community sit close together. It is good preparation for the conversations that fisheries work eventually turns into.
