Nicothoid Copepod Parasite — Cancrid Rock Crabs

Overview

We document a previously undescribed nicothoid copepod that consumes eggs of Pacific rock crabs (Cancridae). The study pairs field collections with brood-stage–specific intensity estimates, establishing parasite impact during development and highlighting a potential missing piece in crab reproductive ecology.

Quick Facts

  • Role: Researcher, Kuris Lab (UCSB)
  • When: Sep 2021 – present
  • Skills: crab identification, dissections, brood-stage scoring, intensity modeling, figure design
  • Links: Manuscript: (in review)

Background and Questions

  • How frequently do broods show nicothoid infestation, and does intensity vary by brood stage?
  • What are the implications for fecundity loss and year-class strength in fished cancrids?

Methods

  • Collections & ID: targeted sampling of brooding cancrids; species confirmation and morphometrics.
  • Dissections & Scoring: egg mass inspection; stage-specific scoring of parasitism and egg condition.
  • Analysis: intensity/frequency summaries by stage; simple models to approximate egg loss and potential reproductive costs.

Results (preview)

  • Consistent presence of a nicothoid copepod in brooding cancrids across sites.
  • Higher apparent intensity at later brood stages, consistent with cumulative brood exposure.
  • Visual evidence supports egg predation, not just commensal presence.
